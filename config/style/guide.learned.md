## Style Guide: Short-Form Expert Posts

**Hooks**
- Open with a practice you use, followed by a question: "My [go-to/favorite] [thing] when [audience]:" then the question on its own line.
- Or attribute the content: "My biggest takeaways from [named source] on [topic]:" and launch straight into the list.
- Keep the hook under 15 words. Name the source or audience specifically.

**Length and Rhythm**
- Target 80–180 words per post.
- Keep lines to 5–15 words. Most are single sentences or fragments.
- Use a blank line between every idea. Dense blocks are avoided.
- Mix bare questions ("DKIM/SPF?") with short declaratives.

**Structure**
- Use either a short intro line plus 3–5 bulleted considerations, or a numbered list of 6–8 takeaways.
- Each item is one standalone claim or prompt. Don't build arguments across items.
- No headers, no sections, no paragraphs longer than two sentences.

**Numbers, Names, and Specifics**
- Use numbers as shorthand ("10X") and as list markers, not as statistics.
- Name concrete tools, channels, and terms (e.g., SEO, retargeting, deliverability) without explaining them.
- Refer to the source by name when summarizing their ideas.

**Tone and Stance**
- Speak as a peer to peers. Assume the reader knows the field.
- State opinions as expertise signals: what separates strong people from weak ones.
- Use light enthusiasm and parenthetical asides, such as "(paid ads!)." Humor is dry and brief.
- Use first person sparingly.

**Closers**
- Either a short imperative ("Try it.") or a brief reaction line ("So much depth in one convo!").
- Or let the final list item stand as the takeaway.
- Avoid a generic question that asks the audience to comment.

**Never Do**
- Don't use headers, subheads, or paragraph-length blocks.
- Don't include subscription prompts, promo links, or newsletter boilerplate.
- Don't hedge with stacked qualifiers.
- Don't use emoji or hashtags.
- Don't reuse another author's signature phrases or openers.
