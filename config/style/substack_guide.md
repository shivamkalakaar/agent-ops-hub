# Style Guide: Long-Form AI Newsletter

## Voice

- Take a position in the first two sentences and state it plainly.
- Write short declarative sentences. Most paragraphs run one to three sentences. A single-line paragraph is allowed for emphasis.
- Name the company, product, or paper every time. Do not write "a major vendor" or "a recent study."
- Reframe a familiar metric as a different one. Contrast the number a vendor advertises with the number that matters, such as deflection versus closed transactions, or benchmark scores versus cost per completed task.
- Use dry, plain humor only when it lands on a concrete detail, such as the number of deaths in a Minecraft run.
- Address the reader directly with "you" when giving a buying or evaluation test.

## Openings

- Open with a number, a named example, or a claim that can be checked. Do not open with a definition of AI or a general trend.
- State the thesis in the second paragraph at the latest.
- Keep the opening under 120 words before the first section break.
- Do not open with a scene, anecdote, or greeting.

## Sections and pacing

- Give each section one job: one claim, one piece of evidence, one implication.
- Use numbered steps when the piece proposes a method. Give each step a short heading that names the action, then define it in one sentence.
- When describing a process, use a plain arrow list (→) with one step per line, as in a customer call flow or a deployment pipeline.
- Keep sections between 150 and 250 words. Cut any section that restates the previous one.
- Do not add a "background" section. Place context inside the section where the claim needs it.

## Evidence and numbers

- Attach a source to every figure in the same sentence: "from Anthropic's own internal data," "estimated," or "on easy difficulty."
- Include the conditions that limit the number, such as difficulty level, time window, or cost basis.
- Use the precision the source gives. Write "71.3%" if the source gives it, and "about 80%" if it does not. Do not round up to make a point stronger.
- Give every large number a comparison: "6x the hours of the employees directing them" or "half the work week."
- Convert a metric into the unit the reader pays for: dollars per completed task, hours of human review, or transactions closed.
- Do not use "significant," "huge," or "dramatic" in place of a number.

## Endings

- End with what the author thinks will happen, or what a named reader (a PM, an infra team) should do next, and why.
- Do not summarize the piece. Do not add a call to subscribe, share, or comment.

## Never

- Never use hype words: "game-changing," "revolutionary," "unprecedented," "the future of."
- Never accept a vendor's headline metric without asking what it leaves out.
- Never copy phrasing, signature lines, or topic framing from the admired essays.
- Never open with "In today's fast-paced world" or any similar throat-clearing line.
- Never use the agent's old tics: "I'd stop X and start Y", "Next time a vendor…", "Before you sign anything…", "worth sitting with", "that's precisely the point".
- Never write "reportedly" or "according to" in every paragraph: attribute a source once, the first time you use it.
- Never use emojis or bold text inside body paragraphs.
- Never cite a number without its source and its conditions.
- Never exceed three consecutive paragraphs without a concrete example, figure, or named product.
