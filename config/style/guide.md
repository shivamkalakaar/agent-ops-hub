# LinkedIn Short-Post Style Guide

**Length and rhythm**
- Target 80–180 words. Most posts land near 90.
- Lines are short: one sentence per line, often one clause.
- Use a blank line between nearly every beat.
- Lists are numbered, 3–10 items, each a single punchy line.
- Arrows (→) or colons can replace connective words.

**Hook patterns**
- Attributed takeaways: "My biggest takeaways from [named person/role]:" then a numbered list.
- Claim plus reversal: "[Common behavior] is where [money/outcome] actually comes from."
- Superlative with a narrow scope: "[Product] is the best [tool] I've used for one very specific job: [job]."
- Credibility-first: "[Group] started doing [thing] way before you."
- Direct question for the reader: "[Common practice] is the [X]. Here's how you'd [test it]."

**Structure**
- Open with the hook, then 1–3 short supporting beats.
- Middle is either a numbered list or a short run of claims, each followed by a concrete example.
- Keep bullets terse; move nuance into a single sentence.
- Prose beats and lists rarely mix in one post.

**Numbers, names, specifics**
- Name the person, company, tool, or source directly.
- Use exact figures ($349/hour, 40%, 20x) and cite where they came from.
- Specifics anchor the claim; avoid unquantified adjectives.

**Tone and stance**
- Confident and declarative. State the take, then move on.
- First person, with occasional self-reference ("I told every lab...").
- Light humor and mild self-deprecation are fine. Don't hedge the core claim.
- Teach through a named example or question rather than lecturing.

**Closers**
- Imperative ("Go look at your ideas tab.")
- Call to action with a link or resource.
- Comment prompt ("Drop a comment if...").
- Pick one; don't stack a question and a CTA.

**Never**
- Open with a greeting or a "Hot take" label.
- Use more than a couple of emoji.
- Bury the hook under context.
- Summarize the post at the end.
- Hedge with "I think maybe" or "it depends."
