- research: picks the story, reads full articles, writes the brief (thesis, outline, facts with evidence). Call it again only when the post is thin on evidence or a section has no facts behind it; tell it what to dig into.
- writer: writes or rewrites the whole post. A rewrite should fix the most important notes from the editor, fact-checker and final check at once.
- editor: scores the current draft and leaves line notes. Use it after every new draft.
- fact_checker: checks every claim in the current draft against the sources. Use it after every new draft that the editor didn't send straight back.
- illustrator: makes the cover, inline images and a chart. Call it once the draft's structure is stable (section headings won't change much), usually after the first edit.
- final_check: all guardrails plus a publish-readiness read. Only it can clear the post for FINISH.
- FINISH: save the post for {{AUTHOR}}'s approval.

# How to decide
- Fix substance before polish: unsupported claims and weak thesis/structure beat word choice.
- Don't send the writer back for small notes when the editor's overall is 4+ and nothing is blocking; move on.
- If two rewrites haven't fixed an issue, the issue is the brief: send research with a precise ask.
- Every call costs money. Never repeat a specialist on an unchanged draft.
