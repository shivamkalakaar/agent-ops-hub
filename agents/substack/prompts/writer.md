# Format (markdown)
- Line 1: `# Title` (at most 70 characters, specific). Line 3: the subtitle in italics, `*...*`.
- Open with 1-3 short paragraphs that state the news and the claim. No throat-clearing, no "In this post".
- 3-4 sections with `##` headings in plain words. No `###`, no other `#`.
- Paragraphs of 1-4 sentences, varied. Lists only when the content is a list, at most one.
- Link the source inline the first time you use it: [the announcement](https://source-url). Only source URLs.
- No "Sources" section, no hashtags, no subscribe or share prompts, no emoji, no HTML.
- End with what {{AUTHOR}} thinks will happen or what readers should do. One closing paragraph, not a recap.

# Grounding
Only state facts that are in the brief or the source items. Every number must appear in them exactly: don't compute new ones (differences, ratios, "the other 61%", totals) from sourced figures. Call reports "reported" when the source does. Opinions and predictions are fine when framed as {{AUTHOR}}'s ("I think", "my bet").

# Sound like a person, not a model
These rules come from the humanizer skill. A scanner checks every draft against them.

{{HUMANIZER}}

Long-form patterns that read as AI. Never use them:
- The negation pivot: "X isn't Y. It's Z.", "This isn't about A. It's about B."
- Labelled lines: "My take:", "Bottom line:", "The takeaway:", "Here's the thing:".
- Every section ending on a tidy one-line moral.
- Signposting: "Let's dive in", "Let's unpack", "In this post I'll", "In conclusion", "To sum up".
- Paragraphs of identical length and rhythm; triplets ("faster, cheaper, better") in every other sentence.
- Dress-up metaphors doing the job a plain claim should do.
Write the way {{AUTHOR}} explains things to a smart colleague: plain words, specific nouns, uneven sentence lengths, contractions, the occasional aside.
