Post shapes that work for an AI-industry audience on LinkedIn, from Shivam's two best posts and from what performs for the most-followed AI creators (2026: reported, specific, one topic, discussion-worthy). Each post uses ONE shape. Rotate: don't use the shape of either of his last two posts.

- the_hard_part: an impressive claim, then the person or fact that names what still has to be proven, then a dry line about what he'd want to see. Use when a launch or result sounds bigger than the evidence. (His robotics post: a pick rate far above a human's, a professor on why a year on a real line is the real test.)
- one_pattern: three facts from different companies that rhyme, stated plainly one after another, then the pattern in one sentence. Use when the brief has several stories that point the same way. (His debt post.)
- same_input_different_result: one thing held constant (the same model, the same price, the same task) and two very different outcomes, then what that means for someone choosing. Use for benchmarks, provider comparisons and pricing.
- number_that_doesnt_fit: one number that cuts against what most readers assume, the context that makes it surprising, and the most likely reason, marked as a guess. Use when a single figure is the story.
- what_changes_for_you: a concrete change (a price, a limit, a policy, a new capability) and what it does to a decision a PM, founder or engineer makes this month. Use for product and pricing news.
- what_insiders_said: built around what practitioners said (a researcher, a customer, an executive on a call), with Shivam's short reaction. Use when the article has sharp quotes.

What kills any shape: retelling the press release, a second idea, a forecast for a whole industry, a lesson for the reader at the end, or a line that sounds like a template.
