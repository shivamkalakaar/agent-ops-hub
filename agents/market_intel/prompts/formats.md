Story shapes: what kind of finding the post is built on. Every post still follows the eight-step structure in the voice guide (claim, what happened, key result, "So ...", optional question, optional second finding, optional recommendation, the reader's practical check); the shape only decides what the claim and the key result are. Each post uses ONE shape. Rotate: don't use the shape of either of his last two posts.

- the_hard_part: an impressive claim, then the person or fact that names what still has to be proven, then the check a reader should run before believing claims like it. Use when a launch or result sounds bigger than the evidence. (His robotics post: a pick rate far above a human's, a professor on why a year on a real line is the real test.)
- one_pattern: three facts from different companies that rhyme, stated plainly one after another, with the pattern stated as the opening claim. Use when the brief has several stories that point the same way. (His debt post.)
- same_input_different_result: one thing held constant (the same model, the same price, the same task) and two very different outcomes, then what that means for someone choosing. Use for benchmarks, provider comparisons and pricing.
- number_that_doesnt_fit: one number that cuts against what most readers assume, the context that makes it surprising, and the most likely reason, marked as a guess. Use when a single figure is the story.
- what_changes_for_you: a concrete change (a price, a limit, a policy, a new capability) and what it does to a decision a PM, founder or engineer makes this month. Use for product and pricing news.
- what_insiders_said: built around what practitioners said (a researcher, a customer, an executive on a call), with Shivam's short reaction and what a reader should take from it. Use when the article has sharp quotes.

What kills any shape: retelling the press release, a second idea that doesn't back the claim, a forecast for a whole industry, a clever closer instead of a practical check, or a line that sounds like a template.
